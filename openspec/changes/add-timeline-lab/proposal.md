# Proposal: Timeline Lab - Historical Cause & Effect Simulator

## Problem Statement

History education often focuses on memorizing dates and facts rather than understanding the cause-and-effect relationships that drive historical change. Students learn _what_ happened but struggle to understand _why_ events unfolded as they did or how different decisions could have led to different outcomes.

Existing history education tools either present static timelines or overly complex strategy games that obscure the underlying historical mechanisms. There's a gap for an interactive tool that lets learners experiment with historical variables and observe the cascading effects of different choices.

## Proposed Solution

Timeline Lab is a step-based historical simulation that lets users explore pivotal moments in history by adjusting key variables and observing how timelines diverge from actual events. Users select a historical scenario (Industrial Revolution, Roman Empire, Space Race), manipulate policy levers (technology investment, trade policy, military spending, diplomacy), and watch how changes cascade through economics, population, territory, and cultural development.

The simulation advances in discrete time steps (years or decades), with each step calculating the effects of user choices using simplified but historically-grounded cause-and-effect models. Visual feedback shows how the alternate timeline diverges from actual history, highlighting the interconnectedness of historical forces.

## Key Features

- **Historical Scenarios**: Pre-configured starting conditions for major historical periods
- **Policy Variables**: Technology investment, trade policy, military spending, diplomatic approach, cultural priorities
- **Cascading Effects**: Changes ripple through multiple dimensions (economy, population, territory, culture)
- **Timeline Visualization**: Side-by-side comparison of user timeline vs. actual history
- **Counterfactual Analysis**: Explore "what if" scenarios with historical grounding

## Success Criteria

- Users can complete 4 missions that demonstrate understanding of cause-and-effect
- Timeline visualization clearly shows divergence from actual history
- Policy changes produce historically plausible cascading effects
- Educational value is evident through mission completion and exploration
- Simulation runs smoothly in step-based mode with responsive controls
